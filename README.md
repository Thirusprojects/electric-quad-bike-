# Electric Quad Bike

A collaborative campus electric vehicle project focused on battery systems, electrical wiring, motor control, and basic vehicle testing.

## Project overview

This was an open campus project where students could participate and gain hands-on experience with an electric vehicle. I contributed during my **second and third year** as part of the team.

**My role: Team Member — Battery Management, Wiring and Motor Control**

The quad bike was a team effort. My contribution focused on the electrical system and learning how the battery, controller, throttle, and motor work together; I did not lead the team.

## My contribution

### Battery management and power connections

- Participated in battery-related work and electrical wiring.
- Developed an understanding of the battery connections and power supply to the motor controller.
- Learned basic battery safety and the role of the battery management system (BMS) in protection and monitoring.

### Wiring and motor control

- Contributed to wiring-related work as a team member.
- Learned about the connections between the BLDC motor, compatible controller, and throttle.
- Gained an understanding of throttle integration and how throttle input regulates motor speed.

### Functional testing

- Participated in the driving demonstration and basic functional checks.
- Helped check throttle response, acceleration, braking performance, and overall vehicle operation.
- Reverse operation and battery protection were considered as part of the system checks where applicable; completed validation of these functions is not documented here.

## System overview

The battery supplies electrical power to the motor controller, which controls the BLDC motor in response to throttle input. The BMS supports battery protection and monitoring.

This description explains the system at a functional level; it is not a verified wiring diagram.

## Learning outcomes

- Understanding electric drivetrain integration.
- Familiarity with battery connections, basic battery safety, and BMS functions.
- Practical exposure to electrical wiring and motor-controller connections.
- Understanding throttle-based motor speed control.
- Experience with basic vehicle checks and collaborative engineering work.

## Project scope

This repository documents my participation in a shared campus learning project. It does not claim sole design ownership, team leadership, custom controller programming, or BMS design and installation.

Testing described here consists of basic functional checks and a driving demonstration. No measured range, top speed, efficiency, or formal safety-validation results are claimed.
